# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a news item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful information about mainland China or Hong Kong equities, the companies listed there, or the policy and capital flows that move them.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Nationwide policy shifts, regulatory overhauls, or market-wide events that reprice Chinese equities broadly, including major moves in index-level liquidity, trading rules, or cross-border capital channels.
- **7-8: Important.** Material earnings surprises, major orders or capacity announcements, consequential regulatory actions against a sector, large financing or M&A, or foreign institution rating changes on Chinese names with a stated rationale.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible, concrete company or sector developments that help readers understand a Chinese business or theme but have limited breadth or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine index moves, expected results, small transactions, weakly supported forecasts, or price commentary without a meaningful baseline.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumors, stock-touting, unexplained numbers, sensational predictions, or content with no reliable financial substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider magnitude and breadth, source quality, timeliness, and whether the item gives concrete figures with meaningful baselines. Distinguish reported facts from forecasts and opinions. Do not reward a large percentage move without its starting value, and do not treat market popularity or a single analyst's target price as economic importance.

# Reader priorities

The reader tracks A-share themes and Hong Kong listings directly, with a focus on AI, computing power, semiconductors, optical modules, servers, and the companies supplying them. Within the 7-10 bands, give the benefit of the doubt to:

- **A-share thematic catalysts.** Policy signals, regulatory changes, major orders, capacity or supply announcements, and company disclosures that could plausibly reprice themes such as AI applications, computing power, optical modules, servers, or domestic semiconductors. State the affected theme when you score it.
- **Foreign institution calls on China.** Rating changes, target-price revisions, and view shifts by international banks or asset managers on Chinese equities or mainland technology names, especially when the rationale is stated.
- **Cross-border capital and policy.** Southbound and northbound flows, Stock Connect changes, IPO and refinancing rules, and regulatory actions with a clear read-through to listed companies.
- **Overseas read-through.** Price hikes, capacity additions, shortages, or export restrictions by non-Chinese suppliers when they plausibly shift pricing power, orders, or market share toward Chinese peers or supply-chain names. Score 5-8 when the transmission path is concrete (same product, same customers, substitutable capacity, or a named Chinese beneficiary); score low when the link is only generic sector sentiment.

These preferences shift scores within the 7-10 band. Keep the credibility bar: reported facts must stay distinguishable from forecasts, market chatter, and unverified retellings, and rumor or stock-touting content should not score high merely because it is topical.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
