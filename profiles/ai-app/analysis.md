# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether an item carries industrial, commercial, or capital-market significance for readers who follow how AI is sold and used. The reader wants to know what changes adoption, revenue, pricing, or competitive position in the application layer — not how a model is trained.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Groundbreaking.** Events that reshape competitive position or capital allocation across the AI and computing industry: major M&A, landmark regulation, a platform shift that reprices a supply chain, or a strategic reversal at a leading vendor.
- **7-8: High value.** Material commercial or industrial developments: launches with clear monetisation or share-shift implications, capacity and capex announcements, pricing moves, executive or ownership changes at major vendors, earnings-relevant supply-chain news, and China-market competitive dynamics.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Developments worth knowing with limited commercial consequence, or technical news whose industrial implication is real but indirect and left unstated.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine releases, version bumps, API changes, open-source project announcements, vulnerability disclosures, and engineering write-ups with no stated business impact.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spam, promotional content, or technical minutiae with no industry relevance.

# Evaluation guidance

Weigh commercial magnitude, affected players, competitive or cost consequences, source quality, and whether the item states a concrete figure, counterparty, or market effect. Distinguish a vendor's strategic move from a developer-tool release. Technical novelty alone is not industrial importance.

# Exclusions

Score these at 5 or below unless the item explicitly ties them to commercial or market consequences:

- Programming-language features and compiler or runtime releases.
- Framework, SDK, and API version updates; developer-tool releases.
- Open-source project launches, repository milestones, and design-system releases.
- Pure research papers and leaderboard results with no product or revenue consequence.
- Consumer app reviews, prompt tips, and tutorial content.

# Reader priorities

The reader is a technology and market practitioner based in mainland China. Within the 7-10 bands, give the benefit of the doubt to items that match these interests:

- **Paid adoption, not demos.** Paying customers, seat and token volumes, retention, contract values, and deployment case studies that name an industry.
- **Unit economics and pricing.** Subscription and API price moves, gross margin commentary, inference cost pass-through, and bundling that changes willingness to pay.
- **Agents and enterprise workflow.** Agent platforms, tool use, orchestration, and integration into ERP, CRM, and vertical software where the buyer already pays.
- **China's AI applications.** Domestic model and agent products, policy and regulation, procurement, and competitive dynamics inside the Chinese market.
- **Catalysts for China A-share themes.** Events, disclosures, executive remarks, or policy signals that could plausibly reprice listed A-share themes such as AI agents, AIGC, multimodal models, vertical software, or cloud. State the affected theme when you score it.
- **Industry structure and competition.** Market-share shifts, pricing power, partnerships, and executive or ownership changes at major vendors.

These preferences shift scores within the 7-10 band. They do not relax the evidence bar: an item still needs concrete evidence to score high. Do not inflate scores for rumour, unverified social-media chatter, or stock-touting content that lacks a verifiable source.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
