# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a financial news item gives a market investor timely, credible, and materially useful information about the economy, markets, companies, or policy — specifically information that can move the price of a major equity market, a listed company, or a technology supply chain.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Major monetary or fiscal policy shifts, severe market disruptions, landmark regulation, or company events with broad economic consequences across markets.
- **7-8: Important.** Material earnings surprises, major financing or acquisition activity, consequential economic data, or policy changes with a clear effect on an industry, a major index, or a large group of listed companies.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible and concrete developments that help readers understand a company, market, or economic trend but have limited breadth or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine price moves, expected results, small transactions, weakly supported forecasts, or reports that lack a meaningful comparison or baseline.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumours, promotional investment claims, unexplained numbers, sensational predictions, or content with no reliable financial substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider the magnitude and breadth of the event, source quality, timeliness, affected groups, and whether the item provides concrete figures with meaningful baselines. Distinguish reported facts from forecasts and opinions. Do not reward a large percentage move without considering the starting value, and do not treat market popularity as economic importance.

Ask one question before scoring above 5: **can an investor price this?** If the item names no listed company, no index, no sector cost structure and no supply chain link, it is a business story, not market news, and belongs at 5 or below no matter how widely it was reported.

# Reader priorities

The reader is a financial-market investor tracking the major equity markets — **mainland China first, then the United States, Japan, Korea and Taiwan** — and is sensitive to AI hardware, AI applications, their supply chains, and other technology themes currently repricing listed names. Within the 7-10 bands, give the benefit of the doubt to:

- **A-share thematic catalysts.** Policy signals, regulatory changes, major orders, capacity or supply announcements, and company disclosures that could plausibly reprice A-share themes such as AI applications, computing power, optical modules, servers, or domestic semiconductors. State the affected theme when you score it.
- **AI industry economics.** Model pricing, inference cost trends, capital expenditure by cloud and chip vendors, and monetisation data that bear on the AI supply chain.
- **US, Japan, Korea and Taiwan market drivers.** Rate decisions, index-level moves, earnings from mega-cap technology names, semiconductor cycle data, export controls, and currency moves that transmit into Asian equities or into the AI supply chain.
- **Cross-market transmission.** Events in one market that plausibly reprice another: a US technology selloff that sets Asia's next open, a Japanese equipment maker's orders that read through to Chinese fabs, a Taiwan foundry's utilisation that moves the whole chain. State the transmission path when you score it.
- **Industry stories inside the tracked industries.** When an item is about an industry rather than the macro, it earns a place here only if the industry is one this digest already tracks:
  - **AI hardware and compute** — accelerators and chips, servers and racks, data-centre capacity and construction, power generation and grid equipment, cooling, optical and copper interconnect, advanced packaging, memory, semiconductor equipment and materials, and the capital spending behind them.
  - **AI applications** — model and agent products, enterprise AI deployment, pricing and unit economics, vertical AI in healthcare, finance, education, media and industry, and consumer AI services.
  - **Other technology industries** — consumer electronics; brain-computer interfaces; automotive technology; automotive manufacturing; financial technology; cybersecurity; telecommunications (5G/6G, spectrum, carriers, satellite, and networking equipment outside the AI data-centre stack); AI in healthcare; robotics and embodied AI.

  An industry story outside that list — restaurant or retail sales, steel, cement, property, agriculture, utilities, general manufacturing — needs a stated price transmission into a major index, a large listed peer group, or one of the tracked industries before it clears 5.

Score **5 or below** regardless of how widely an item was covered, unless it states one of those links:

- Business operations at a single consumer brand, retailer, restaurant chain or non-listed company with no read-through to a major index, a listed peer, or a technology chain.
- Labour, HR, workplace, or lifestyle stories that do not change an earnings outlook or a sector's cost structure in a priceable way.
- Sector operations or consolidation in an industry outside that list — a restaurant chain's traffic, a steelmaker buying a domestic peer, a cement or property developer's sales — reported without a stated effect on a major index constituent, a broad listed peer group, or one of the tracked industries.

These preferences shift scores within the 7-10 band. Keep the existing credibility bar: reported facts must stay distinguishable from forecasts and market chatter, and unverified rumour or stock-touting content should not score high merely because it is topical.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
